## 2. What MyDNA is

MyDNA is a research and educational tool. It queries public biomedical databases
and uses an AI model to explain what they contain, in plain language, with the
sources shown.

## 3. Not medical advice

**This is the most important term here.**

MyDNA does **not** provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. It is not a
medical device, a clinical decision support tool, or a substitute for a
qualified professional. Nothing it produces is a diagnosis, prognosis, risk
assessment, or recommendation about your health.

**Speak to your clinician about your personal situation.** Do not start, stop
or change any treatment, test or medication because of anything you read here.
In an emergency, contact emergency services.

Where MyDNA names a clinical genetic test that exists for a gene, that is
**information from a public registry, not a recommendation** that you take it.
We have no relationship with any testing laboratory and receive nothing if you
contact one.

Genetic information is difficult to interpret correctly, and carrying a variant
is not the same as having or developing a condition. Interpretation depends on
family history, clinical context and other factors that a raw data file cannot
supply. A qualified genetic counsellor or clinician is the right person to help
with that.

**This applies most of all when you combine your own data with your own
reading.** Uploading your DNA alongside a paper about a condition can make an
answer feel like it is about you. It is not. MyDNA describes relationships in
published data — which variants are associated with which phenotypes, in whose
cohort, at what strength — and declines to draw a conclusion about any
individual. Where a question asks for one, it will say so and give you the
underlying relationship instead. That is a deliberate limit, not a gap to be
worked around by rephrasing.

## 4. Accuracy, and its limits

MyDNA draws on public databases maintained by others. We do not control their
accuracy, completeness or availability, and their contents change.

Answers are written by a large language model. **It can be wrong.** It can
misread a source, overstate a finding, or omit relevant context. Every answer
names the sources it used and links to the original records specifically so you
can check it, and you should treat that as part of using the tool rather than an
optional extra.

MyDNA is provided on an "as is" and "as available" basis, with no warranty of
accuracy, fitness for a particular purpose, or uninterrupted availability.
